# Statewide Overview

New Hampshire does **not** have a western-style system of broad BLM boondocking.

The primary free primitive camping opportunities are within the **White Mountain National Forest (WMNF)**.

The most practical verified roadside areas for a car-based nomad setup are:

- Haystack Road
- Gale River Loop Road
- Cherry Mountain Road
- Jefferson Notch Road
- Town Hall Road system
- Elbow Pond Road

There is also extensive legal backcountry dispersed camping throughout portions of the White Mountain National Forest, but much of it requires hiking away from roads and therefore is less useful as a vehicle-based nomad camp.

Several roads are covered by **Forest Protection Area (FPA)** rules, meaning you cannot simply camp in any random roadside pull-off. In those areas, camping is restricted to signed or designated campsites.

# Universal White Mountain National Forest Rules

## Stay Limit

A practical working rule for WMNF dispersed camping is:

**Up to 14 days within a 30-day period**

Some individual roadside areas explicitly state 14-day limits.

Because special orders can change, verify with the responsible ranger district before planning a full two-week stay.

---

## Forest Protection Areas

Forest Protection Areas can prohibit camping and fires within specified distances of:

- Roads
- Trails
- Trailheads
- Shelters
- Huts
- Water bodies
- Developed recreation facilities
- Other heavily used areas

Typical restricted distances are:

- **200 feet**
- or **1/4 mile**, depending on the specific protection order

Designated campsites inside a protection area may still be legal when marked for camping.

### Rule of thumb

If a road has signed or numbered campsites, **use those sites rather than making your own campsite**.

---

## Human Waste / Poop Disposal

Where burial is legally allowed and no toilet exists:

1. Choose a location at least **200 feet from water, camp, and trails**.
2. Dig a cathole approximately **6–8 inches deep**.
3. Bury human waste completely.
4. Pack out toilet paper, wipes, and hygiene products.

### Best choice for a car nomad

A portable toilet with:

- waste bags
- absorbent gel/powder
- sealed disposal bags

is cleaner and avoids repeatedly digging catholes around popular roadside camping areas.

---

## Campfire Rules

When fires are permitted:

- Prefer an existing established fire ring.
- Use only dead-and-down wood where collection is permitted.
- Do not cut live trees.
- Never leave a fire unattended.
- Drown, stir, and cool the fire completely before leaving.
- Obey all temporary fire bans and Forest Orders.

### Alpine zone

Campfires are prohibited in the alpine zone.

---

## Food Storage

Black bears are present throughout the region.

Store:

- food
- trash
- toothpaste
- deodorant
- scented toiletries
- cooking equipment
- pet food

in a bear-resistant manner.

The Pemigewasset Wilderness has an additional mandatory bear-canister rule described later in this guide.

# 1. Haystack Road

**Road:** Haystack Road / Forest Road 304  
**General area:** Twin Mountain / White Mountain National Forest  
**Camping type:** Numbered free roadside primitive campsites  
**Nomad suitability:** Excellent

Haystack Road is one of the strongest free camping choices in New Hampshire for a low-clearance vehicle and a long-stay nomad setup.

Approximately **11 numbered free dispersed campsites** are available.

Reported site limits include:

- Up to 14 days
- Up to 15 people
- Up to 3 vehicles per site

---

## 🚗 Hyundai Accent Access

### Rating: ⭐⭐⭐⭐½ / 5

Generally:

- Passenger-car friendly
- Dirt/gravel road
- Easier near the highway
- More wooded farther in

Potential problems include:

- Potholes
- Mud
- Standing water
- Soft shoulders
- Washouts after storms

### Recommended strategy

If a puddle, rut, or muddy entrance looks questionable:

1. Stop.
2. Park safely.
3. Walk it.
4. Inspect depth and firmness.
5. Only drive through if clearly safe.

# 4. Jefferson Notch Road

**General area:** Jefferson / Mount Washington region  
**Camping type:** Limited dispersed roadside camping outside protected sections  
**Nomad suitability:** Moderate

A significant legal restriction applies here.

Camping restrictions cover the road from **Base Station Road north to the Caps Ridge Trailhead**.

Therefore, dispersed roadside camping should be sought only in areas **outside the restricted protection zone**, particularly north of the Caps Ridge area where legal.

- Gravel road
- Narrow mountain terrain

Avoid:

- steep southern sections
- heavily rutted conditions

# Backcountry Dispersed Camping

The six roadside areas above are not the only places where free camping exists.

Large sections of White Mountain National Forest allow dispersed backcountry camping where:

- no special protection order prohibits it
- distance requirements are followed
- wilderness regulations are respected

These areas may contain thousands of possible legal tent locations.

---

## Important Distinction

### Vehicle nomad camp

You:

- drive directly to camp
- unload gear next to the car
- use the car for resupply
- can leave quickly during weather emergencies

### Backcountry dispersed camp

You:

- park legally
- hike away from the road
- carry equipment
- camp beyond required setback distances

---

## Nomad Suitability

**Vehicle-based nomad use:** ⭐ / 5

**Backpacking / solitude:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Backcountry camping is excellent for short wilderness retreats but inefficient as a full nomad base.

# Pemigewasset Wilderness Bear-Canister Rule

A major rule change took effect **May 1, 2026**.

Overnight visitors entering the **Pemigewasset Wilderness** must store:

- food
- garbage
- toiletries
- scented items

inside a:

**Cylindrical, hard-sided, non-pliable, IGBC-certified bear-resistant canister**

---

## Bear Hangs

Traditional suspended bear bags **do not satisfy the rule**.

---

## Canister Rentals

Forest Service visitor centers in the region may offer approved canisters for loan or rental.

Relevant visitor-center areas include:

- Lincoln
- Campton
- Conway
- Gorham

Verify availability before depending on one.

# New Hampshire State Forests and State Parks

State lands operate differently from White Mountain National Forest.

You generally **cannot create your own roadside dispersed campsite on New Hampshire state property**.

Camping is restricted to:

- designated campsites
- shelters
- cabins
- yurts
- other officially authorized overnight facilities

---

## Remote State Shelters

Some legal remote shelters exist along routes such as:

- Cohos Trail
- Monadnock-Sunapee Greenway
- areas within Nash Stream State Forest
- Max Israel State Forest
- Pillsbury State Park

These are primarily backpacking facilities.

Some remote sites restrict stays to approximately **two consecutive nights**.

---

## State Park Primitive Campsites

“Primitive” does **not** mean free.

New Hampshire State Parks charge nightly camping fees, so they should not be included in a strict free-camping database.

---

# Places Commonly Mistaken for Free Camping

## Tripoli Road

Tripoli Road frequently appears on old dispersed-camping lists.

However, roadside camping there is **fee-based**, not free.

Therefore:

**Do not classify Tripoli Road as free primitive camping.**

---

## Developed WMNF Campgrounds

Many Forest Service campgrounds may look primitive but charge nightly fees.

Examples should be kept separate from a true zero-fee camping list.

---

## AMC / RMC Facilities

Appalachian Mountain Club and Randolph Mountain Club shelters, cabins, or campsites may require fees or donations.

They are not equivalent to unrestricted free dispersed camping.

# Best Two-Camp Nomad Rotation

A particularly efficient setup is:

## Camp 1
**Haystack Road**

Stay up to the legal limit.

Then:

- resupply in Twin Mountain or Littleton
- dump trash
- refill water
- charge batteries
- perform laundry
- use library Wi-Fi if needed

Then move to:

## Camp 2
**Gale River Loop**

This keeps both camps within essentially the same regional service ecosystem.

Benefits:

- low fuel consumption
- familiar grocery stores
- familiar library
- familiar auto services
- little time wasted learning new towns
- simple emergency planning
