- Legal stay limits

- Campfire rules

- Ranger and agency contacts
- Road conditions
- 2014 Hyundai Accent SE accessibility

> **Important:** Florida camping rules, hunt closures, fire bans, road conditions, and permit systems can change. Always verify with the managing agency immediately before travel.

# Agency-Wide Rules

## U.S. Forest Service — National Forests in Florida
**Typical stay rule:** Many Florida National Forest recreation zones use approximately **14 consecutive days in a 30-day period**, but forest orders and local rules control.

**Campfires**
- Subject to fire restrictions and local forest orders.
- Never leave a fire unattended.
- Use established sites whenever possible.

**Human waste**
- Use toilets where provided.
- In legal backcountry settings, use Leave No Trace methods.
- Pack-out systems are preferable in wet or heavily used areas.

**Contacts**
- Apalachicola Ranger District: **850-643-2282**
- Ocala — Lake George Ranger District: **352-625-2520**
- Ocala — Seminole Ranger District: **352-669-3153**
- Osceola Ranger District: **386-752-2577**

---

## Southwest Florida Water Management District — SWFWMD
**Typical stay rule:** **7 consecutive days, 30 days per year.**

**Campfires**
- Small fires in designated rings where allowed.
- Burn bans override all normal rules.

**Human waste**
- Many drive-in primitive campgrounds provide portable toilets.
- At sites without toilets, a portable toilet or WAG-bag system is the safest practice.

**Contact**
- **800-423-1476**
- **352-796-7211 ext. 4470**

---

## St. Johns River Water Management District — SJRWMD
**Typical stay rule:** **7 consecutive days, 30 days per property per year.**

**Campfires**
- Designated fire rings only.
- Use dead and down wood where permitted.
- Burn bans override normal rules.

**Human waste**
- Use provided toilets where available.
- Pack-out is recommended at sites without toilets unless site-specific rules clearly allow catholes.

**Contact**
- **386-329-4401**

---

## South Florida Water Management District — SFWMD
**Typical stay rule:** Current planning guidance allows approximately **8 consecutive days per permit**, then at least one full day out, with a **30-day annual District-wide limit**.

**Campfires**
- Site and Special Use License conditions control.
- Use designated fire rings where present.

**Human waste**
- Some backpacking sites explicitly permit burial at least 6 inches deep and away from camp and water.
- Other locations should be treated as pack-out unless their site rules say otherwise.

**Contact**
- Recreation: **866-433-6312**

---

## Suwannee River Water Management District — SRWMD

# 1. Apalachicola National Forest

## Overview
Large and genuinely remote North Florida forest with extensive unpaved roads, wetlands, pine flatwoods, and dispersed camping opportunities.

**Nearest supply bases**
- Tallahassee
- Crawfordville
- Bristol
- Sopchoppy

**Agency**
- U.S. Forest Service
- Apalachicola Ranger District: **850-643-2282**

### Ratings
| Factor | Rating |
|---|---|
| Hyundai Accent | 3/5 dry, 2/5 wet |
| Terrain | 3/5 |
| Road grade | 5/5 |
| Amenities | 2–3/5 |
| Supply pain | 3–4/5 |
| Nomad connectivity | 2–3/5 |
| Verizon | 3/5 |
| AT&T | 3/5 |
| T-Mobile | 2/5 |
| Distance to town/corridor | Roughly 5–25 miles depending on camp area |

### Road Conditions
The problem is not hills. The problem is:
- Standing water
- Mud
- Washed-out forest roads
- Soft shoulders
- Deep ruts

Stay on legal primary Forest Service roads shown on current motor-vehicle-use maps.

### Seasonal Conditions
**Best:** November–April

**Most difficult:** Summer through early fall

### Hazards
- Flooding
- Mosquitoes
- Ticks
- Snakes
- Black bears
- Wild hogs
- Hunting
- Wildfire
- Prescribed fire

### Nomad Verdict
Excellent solitude but mediocre as a full-time cellular work base.

# Hyundai Accent Florida Rule

> **Never drive through water when you cannot see the bottom.**

A shallow-looking puddle can hide:
- Deep ruts
- Washed-out roadbed
- Mud holes
- Sharp debris
- Enough depth to flood a low-mounted intake or underbody component

Florida's greatest road hazard is usually not grade. It is water and loose ground.

# Final Nomad Takeaway

Florida is strongest for this lifestyle during the dry season.

For a low-clearance Hyundai Accent, the best strategy is:

- Favor official drive-in primitive campgrounds first
- Use National Forest dispersed camping selectively
- Avoid deep sugar-sand roads
- Avoid unknown flooded roads
- Check hunt closures before every stay
- Check burn bans before lighting any fire
- Keep a pack-out toilet system available
- Treat cellular coverage as site-specific, not guaranteed
- Stay closer to major road corridors when internet is important
- Save the remote swamps and WMA interiors for offline periods or later experience

The Tampa–Ocala–I-75 corridor is probably the best overall Florida region for combining **free primitive camping, reliable resupply, libraries, gyms, reasonable cellular internet, and manageable Hyundai Accent access**.
