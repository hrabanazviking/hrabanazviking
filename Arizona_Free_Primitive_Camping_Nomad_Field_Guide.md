# 1. Arizona Primitive Camping Overview

Arizona is one of the strongest U.S. states for free primitive camping because of its enormous network of:

- BLM land
- National Forest land
- National Conservation Areas
- National Wildlife Refuge primitive areas
- Designated dispersed camping zones
- Long-established forest-road and desert-road camping corridors

Unlike Iowa, Arizona has millions of acres where dispersed camping can be legal.

That does **not** mean every dirt road or open-looking patch is automatically legal.

Always verify:

- agency ownership
- current Motor Vehicle Use Map or travel map
- active fire restrictions
- temporary area closures
- road condition
- local stay limits
- whether camping must be in designated sites

# 2. Major Stay-Limit Rules

## Bureau of Land Management

General Arizona BLM dispersed camping rule:

**14 days within a 28-day period**, unless an individual area has different rules.

After reaching the limit, local movement requirements may apply.

## Kaibab National Forest

**14 days within a 30-day period**

## Coconino National Forest

**14 days within a consecutive 30-day period**

## Apache-Sitgreaves National Forests

**14 consecutive days within a 30-day period**

## Prescott National Forest

Most forest areas:

**14 days**

Prescott Basin designated dispersed sites:

**7 days within 30 days**

## Tonto National Forest

Forest occupancy is generally limited to:

**14 days within a 90-day period**

## Kofa National Wildlife Refuge

Much stricter:

**14 days total within any 12-month period**

# 12. Kofa National Wildlife Refuge

Kofa offers free primitive camping but is much stricter and much rougher than ordinary BLM land.

## Stay Limit

**14 days total within any 12-month period**

## Camping Rules

- Camp at least 1/4 mile from water sources
- Vehicles must remain within approximately 100 feet of designated roads
- Use only legal established travel routes
- No off-road vehicle wandering

## Campfires

Use only:

- dead
- down
- detached wood

Follow all fire restrictions.

## 📶 Connectivity

**Rating:** ⭐ / 5

- T-Mobile: Poor / none
- Verizon: Poor / none
- AT&T: Poor / none
- Terrain obstruction: High
- Internet dependability: No

## Accent Access

**4-5/5**

Many roads are:

- rocky
- brushy
- sandy
- unmaintained

The Hyundai Accent is **not** a good vehicle for deep refuge roads.

## Best Strategy

Camp only near road sections confirmed by refuge staff as passenger-car safe.

## Supplies

Possible supply bases:

- Quartzsite
- Yuma
- Highway 95 corridor

## Supply Pain

**4/5**

## Best Season

November-March

## Major Hazards

- extreme heat
- deep sand
- rock damage
- flash floods
- rattlesnakes
- no cellular service
- very long recovery times

Official page:  
https://www.fws.gov/refuge/kofa

# 45. Arizona Campfire Rules

Fire legality changes constantly.

Always check the current statewide interagency fire-restriction map.

Official source:  
https://wildlandfire.az.gov/fire-restrictions

## No Restrictions

Normal agency rules apply.

## Stage 1

Common restrictions include:

- dispersed wood fires prohibited
- charcoal restricted
- fires limited to developed sites
- propane devices with shutoff valves often remain legal

## Stage 2

Typically:

- wood fires prohibited
- charcoal prohibited
- even many developed-site fires prohibited
- controlled propane devices may remain legal

## Stage 3

Area closure.

## Always Prohibited on Federal Arizona Lands

- fireworks
- exploding targets
- incendiary devices

- lake shores

# 48. Major Arizona Hazards

## 1. Extreme Heat

Low desert temperatures can exceed 110°F.

Carry large water reserves.

Never rely on one small container.

## 2. Flash Floods

Do not camp in washes.

Rain miles away can create dangerous downstream flooding.

## 3. Wildfire

Fire restrictions can change quickly.

## 4. Lightning

Particularly dangerous during summer monsoon season in high country.

## 5. Mud

Forest roads can become impassable after storms.

## 6. Deep Sand

Common in western desert roads.

## 7. Wildlife

Potentially includes:

- rattlesnakes
- scorpions
- black bears
- mountain lions
- elk
- cattle

## 8. Thorn Damage

Cactus and sharp desert vegetation can damage tires.

## 9. Winter Hazards

High-elevation Arizona can experience:

- snow
- ice
- road closures
- black ice
- subfreezing nights

# 55. Final Arizona Nomad Verdict

Arizona is one of the strongest states in the country for free primitive nomad camping.

Its biggest strengths are:

- huge public-land inventory
- winter desert camping
- summer mountain camping
- many legal 14-day rotations
- excellent cellular service in several key areas
- very easy desert roads in places like Quartzsite and Gunsight Wash
- excellent major resupply cities scattered around the state
- climate control through elevation changes

The best overall pattern for a slow Hyundai Accent nomad is:

1. **Winter:** Quartzsite / Yuma / Ajo
2. **Early spring:** Sonoran Desert / Las Cienegas / Sedona
3. **Late spring:** Prescott / Kaibab
4. **Summer:** Flagstaff / Mogollon Rim / White Mountains / Mt. Graham
5. **Fall:** Prescott / Sedona / Las Cienegas
6. **Late fall:** return to Quartzsite / Ajo / Yuma

The biggest discipline required is simple:

**do not follow high-clearance rigs down roads just because they look fun.**

Arizona has far too many good legal camps to sacrifice the Accent's oil pan to curiosity.
