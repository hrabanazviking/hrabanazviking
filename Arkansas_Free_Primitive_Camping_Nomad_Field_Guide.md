# Quick Summary

Arkansas is one of the strongest states in the region for free primitive camping.

Its biggest advantages are:

- Huge areas of National Forest
- Generous dispersed-camping limits
- Excellent winter-through-spring migration possibilities
- Free primitive camps in multiple regions
- Buffalo National River free sites
- Many forest roads usable by ordinary passenger cars when dry
- Large regional supply towns such as Hot Springs, Russellville, Harrison, Mountain Home, Booneville, Camden, and Mena

Its biggest disadvantages are:

- Steep Ozark terrain
- Frequent cellular dead zones
- Flash flooding
- Low-water crossings
- Rough gravel roads
- Soft Buffalo River gravel bars
- Rapid road deterioration after storms
- Winter ice on mountain roads

For a **2014 Hyundai Accent SE**, Arkansas is highly usable if road decisions are made based on **current weather and surface conditions**, not just what an app says.

---

# What Counts as Free

This guide treats **free** as:

> **No nightly camping fee**

Places requiring a genuinely free permit are identified separately.

Arkansas Game and Fish Commission WMAs generally do **not** count as universally free because adults on many AGFC-owned WMAs need:

- A hunting/fishing license, or
- A WMA/Lake Conservation Permit

Some cooperatively managed lands have different permit rules.

# Ouachita National Forest

Ouachita National Forest may be the strongest Arkansas region for a small-car slow-nomad setup.

It combines:

- Long dispersed-camping limits
- Hot Springs / Mount Ida / Mena / Booneville supply hubs
- Ridge camps with usable cellular service
- Many roads that are easier than the deepest Ozark terrain

---

## Ouachita General Dispersed Camping Stay Rule

Current Ouachita forest orders generally allow:

- **Up to 30 consecutive days** in the General Forest
- Then the campsite must be removed for at least **14 days**
- Camp cannot simply be re-established immediately nearby
- Annual total may be limited to approximately **90 days**

Always verify district-specific orders before a long stay.

# 1. Hickory Nut Mountain

## Overview

One of the strongest Arkansas choices for a digitally connected forest camp.

**Cost:** Free  
**Stay:** Verify campground-specific limit  
**Type:** Drive-in mountaintop primitive campground

### Ratings

- **📶 Nomad Connectivity:** 4/5
- **🚗 Accent Road:** 3/5 dry, 4/5 wet
- **🏔️ Terrain:** 4/5
- **🛒 Services:** 3.5/5
- **🔁 Supply Pain:** 2.5/5

### Carrier Expectations

- **T-Mobile:** Fair
- **Verizon:** Fair
- **AT&T:** Good

Current user reports indicate AT&T may be the strongest option.

### Terrain Obstruction

Lower than most hollow/creek camps because of the **ridgetop location**.

This makes Hickory Nut significantly better for cellular internet than many nearby low-elevation camps.

### Road

Expect:

- Narrow gravel
- Sustained climbing
- Possible washboarding
- Ruts after storms

### Supply Towns

- **Mount Ida:** practical nearby resupply
- **Hot Springs:** major groceries, library, gym, medical, laundry, shopping

### Hazards

- Steep road
- Slick gravel after rain
- Winter ice
- Falling limbs
- Severe thunderstorms

### Nomad Verdict

**One of the best Arkansas forest locations for usable internet plus isolation.**

# Buffalo National River

Buffalo National River provides some of Arkansas's most interesting free primitive camping.

It also introduces one of the most serious hazards:

> **Rapid river rise and gravel-bar flooding**

---

## Buffalo National River Stay Limit

Current NPS rules generally allow:

- **14 days at one campsite**
- Approximately **30 days total per calendar year**

Check current NPS rules before long stays.

---

## Buffalo Human Waste Rules

In non-developed areas, NPS allows:

- Toilets where provided
- Portable/self-contained toilet
- WAG-type pack-out system
- Catholes approximately:
  - **6 to 8 inches deep**
  - At least **200 feet from water**
  - At least **200 feet from camp**
  - At least **200 feet from trails**

Pack out toilet paper.

---

## Buffalo Campfires

Generally:

- Use existing fire rings in campgrounds
- In backcountry, use dead/down wood where permitted
- Temporary fire restrictions override normal rules
- Never cut live vegetation
- Fully extinguish fires

---

## Buffalo Flood Warning

The Buffalo River can rise:

- More than **one foot per hour**
- More than **25 feet in a day**

Rain may occur upstream even when it is not raining at your campsite.

Always have:

- High-ground escape route
- Weather awareness
- Conservative parking location

# Arkansas WMA Rules

Most AGFC-owned WMAs should **not** be treated as universally free camping.

Current rules generally include:

- Camping only in designated areas
- Approximately **14 consecutive days**
- Campers age 16+ need:
  - hunting/fishing license, or
  - WMA/Lake Conservation Permit
- Camps generally cannot remain unoccupied more than 48 hours
- Annual camping may be limited to approximately 30 days

This guide therefore does not pad the free-camping list with ordinary WMAs.

# Human Waste and Campfire Rules

Agency rules vary.

---

## Buffalo National River

Use:

- Developed toilets where available
- Portable/self-contained toilet
- WAG system
- Cathole about 6-8 inches deep
- At least 200 feet from:
  - water
  - camp
  - trails

Pack out toilet paper.

---

## Ouachita National Forest

Use a conservative Leave No Trace standard:

- 200 feet from water
- 200 feet from camp
- 200 feet from trails
- Cathole approximately 6-8 inches deep
- Pack out toilet paper

Portable toilet systems are preferable in:

- Rocky ground
- High-use sites
- Flood-prone terrain
- Creek bottoms

---

## Ozark-St. Francis National Forest

Use:

- Vault toilets where available
- Existing sanitation facilities
- Leave No Trace backcountry standards in dispersed areas

Pack out waste where catholes are impractical.

---

## Campfires

Across agencies:

- Use existing rings where available
- Burn only legal material
- Do not cut live trees
- Keep fires controlled
- Fully extinguish before leaving
- Temporary fire bans override normal rules

At Buffalo backcountry sites, dead/down wood may generally be used where legal.

# Arkansas Hazards

## 1. Flash Flooding

This is one of the most important Arkansas dangers.

Never assume:

- A low-water crossing is safe
- A gravel bar will remain dry
- Current weather at camp reflects upstream rainfall

---

## 2. Low-Water Crossings

A compact car can be moved by relatively shallow fast-moving water.

### Accent Rule

> Never cross moving water because a map says the road continues.

---

## 3. Buffalo River Gravel Bars

Soft gravel can trap the Accent after flooding.

Park on:

- Firm
- High
- Established
- Dry

ground whenever uncertain.

---

## 4. Steep Forest Roads

Descending can be harder than climbing.

Concerns:

- Ground clearance
- Brakes
- Loose gravel
- Sharp drainage dips
- Ruts

---

## 5. Mud and Ruts

Forest-road quality can change overnight.

After major rain:

- Scout first
- Avoid questionable side spurs
- Do not rely on old reviews

---

## 6. Black Bears

Use proper food storage.

Secure:

- Food
- Trash
- Scented toiletries
- Cooking supplies

Follow Buffalo National River food-storage rules where applicable.

---

## 7. Snakes

Potential venomous species include:

- Copperheads
- Cottonmouths
- Rattlesnakes

Use lights at night.

Watch where hands and feet are placed.

---

## 8. Ticks and Chiggers

Arkansas woods can be extremely tick-heavy.

Recommended:

- Repellent
- Long lightweight clothing
- Tick checks
- Keep bedding screened
- Inspect clothing

---

## 9. Cliffs

Especially important at:

- Sam's Throne
- Buffalo bluffs
- Ozark overlooks

Avoid night wandering near edges.

---

## 10. Tornadoes and Severe Thunderstorms

A tent is not tornado shelter.

Know where the nearest:

- Library
- Municipal building
- Store
- Ranger station
- Storm shelter

is before severe weather arrives.

---

## 11. Cell Dead Zones

Download before leaving pavement:

- Offline maps
- Forest maps
- Road layers
- Weather information
- Emergency contacts

---

## 12. Hunting Season

Wear visible clothing when hiking.

Blaze orange is a good idea during active seasons.

---

## 13. Falling Trees and Limbs

Wind, ice, and saturated soil increase tree-fall risk.

Avoid camping directly beneath:

- Dead limbs
- Leaning trees
- Damaged trunks

---

## 14. Fire Restrictions

Check agency restrictions before using open flame.

## Ridge vs Hollow Rule

Arkansas gives you a useful nomad trick:

### Ridge Camp

Better for:

- Cell signal
- Solar
- Wind
- Internet

Better for:

- Solitude
- Shade
- Water access
- Silence

but usually worse for cellular internet.

The mountains themselves become a kind of **connectivity switch**.
