# 1. Pennsylvania Camping Rule That Changes Everything

Pennsylvania's State Forest system contains an enormous amount of free primitive camping, but most of it is **backpack-style primitive camping rather than free roadside vehicle camping**.

DCNR considers camping motorized/roadside camping when the vehicle is close enough to be used for:

- storage
- transportation during the overnight stay
- direct campsite support

Motorized State Forest camping generally requires:

- a designated roadside campsite
- a reservation
- a permit
- a fee

These roadside sites are therefore **not included as free camping in this guide**.

Current roadside rules generally include:

- up to **7 consecutive nights**
- at least **48 hours away** before returning
- resident/nonresident nightly fees

For an Indiana-based traveler, the nonresident rate has been approximately:

**$15/night plus applicable fees**

That makes the State Forest roadside system a different category from true free camping.

---

## Free State Forest primitive camping

The free option is generally backpack-style primitive camping away from the vehicle.

Typical statewide rules include:

- one night at a location normally requires no permit
- more than one night at the same primitive site requires a **free camping permit**
- camping must occur away from roads and developed areas
- special Natural Areas may prohibit camping entirely
- local forest districts may impose additional rules

---

## The practical nomad conclusion

For a traveler who wants to:

- sleep near the Accent
- store gear in the Accent
- use the car as the base camp
- remain somewhere for up to two weeks

the most useful free system in Pennsylvania is:

# **Allegheny National Forest**

The 20 Pennsylvania State Forest districts are far more useful as:

> **park-the-car-and-backpack-away-from-it wilderness systems**

rather than primary vehicle-nomad bases.

# 3. Allegheny National Forest

Allegheny National Forest is Pennsylvania's strongest system for genuine free vehicle-based dispersed camping.

## General stay limit

ANF dispersed camping generally permits:

- up to **14 days at one campsite**
- after 14 days, move at least **3 miles**
- total dispersed camping limited to approximately **28 days in a 60-day period**

Always verify current orders.

---

## Motor Vehicle Use Map

The current **MVUM** determines:

- which Forest Service roads are open
- which routes permit motor vehicles
- where roadside vehicle access is legal
- whether off-road access to a campsite is allowed

Where authorized, short vehicle access away from the road may be permitted, but only without causing resource damage.

---

## Forest Road Maintenance Levels

This is particularly useful for a Hyundai Accent.

### Maintenance Level 2
Intended primarily for:

- high-clearance vehicles
- low-speed administrative/recreation travel

Passenger-car comfort and suitability are **not** a design objective.

### Maintenance Level 3
Maintained for:

- prudent standard passenger-car travel

### Maintenance Levels 4–5
Increasingly better road standards.

## Accent rule

Whenever road maintenance level is known:

> **Prefer ML3 or higher.**

A Level 2 road may still physically be passable, but it is not something to assume safe for a low-clearance Accent.

---

# Allegheny NF Sanitation

For primitive camping:

- use a portable toilet/WAG system when practical
- otherwise dig a cathole approximately **6–8 inches deep**
- remain approximately **200 feet from water, camps, and trails**
- pack toilet paper and hygiene products out
- pack all garbage out

---

# Campfires

Where not prohibited by current fire restrictions:

- use existing fire rings where available
- use dead-and-down wood
- do not cut live vegetation
- dead/down Forest wood may normally be used at camp without a firewood permit
- avoid transporting outside firewood into ANF
- drown and stir the fire until cold before leaving

- camping within 500 feet of portions of **FR401**
- camping within 500 feet of portions of **FR125**

Always check active orders before using any forest road.

# 6. Pennsylvania State Forest System

Pennsylvania operates **20 State Forest districts** containing roughly **2.2 million acres**.

These forests offer enormous free primitive-backpacking potential.

They generally do **not** offer free roadside car camping.

---

## State Forest Primitive-Camping Rules

General rules include:

- one night in one location normally does not require a permit
- more than one night at the same site requires a free permit
- camping is prohibited in certain Natural Areas
- primitive camps should generally be at least **200 feet from a forest road**
- at least **25 feet from a trail**
- at least **100 feet from streams/open water**
- the motor vehicle cannot function as part of the primitive overnight camp

Always verify local district rules.

---

## Human Waste

DCNR Leave No Trace guidance generally recommends:

- cathole approximately **6–8 inches deep**
- approximately **200 feet from water, camp, and trails**
- pack toilet paper and hygiene products out
- dispose of wash water about 200 feet from natural water

---

## Campfires

Open fires are generally prohibited:

**March 1 through May 25**

unless specifically authorized.

Fires may also be prohibited whenever fire danger is:

- high
- very high
- extreme

Always check active restrictions.

# 10. Delaware Water Gap Backcountry

Delaware Water Gap National Recreation Area does **not** provide unrestricted random primitive camping.

Free backcountry camping is largely limited to:

- Appalachian Trail hikers
- multi-night foot travel
- specific backcountry rules

### Fire Rule

Ground fires and charcoal fires are prohibited.

Use:

- self-contained backpacking stoves

### Important Exclusion

Delaware River primitive campsites inside the NRA may charge fees.

Those are not included as free sites in this guide.

# 11. Places That Are Not Free Primitive Camping

---

## Pennsylvania State Game Lands

General recreational camping is **not allowed**.

Do not treat:

- game-land parking areas
- hunting access roads
- trailheads

as stealth camping sites.

---

## Pennsylvania State Parks

Random primitive camping is not allowed.

Backpack sites and shelters are generally designated and may charge fees.

---

## DCNR Roadside State Forest Campsites

Primitive but **not free**.

Typical nonresident rate:

**approximately $15/night plus applicable charges**

This makes them a different economic category from ANF dispersed camping.

## Winter

Common problems:

- snow
- unplowed State Forest roads
- black ice
- frozen ruts
- road gates
- lake-effect snow in northwest PA

ANF and northern State Forest roads should not be assumed passable.

---

## Spring

Often the **worst road season** for the Accent.

Expect:

- thawing roadbeds
- deep mud
- potholes
- runoff
- swollen streams
- washouts

A road that is easy in August may be terrible in March.

---

## Summer

Expect:

- humidity
- thunderstorms
- mosquitoes
- ticks
- occasional high heat

Higher northern plateaus remain cooler.

---

## Fall

Probably Pennsylvania's best general camping season.

Advantages:

- cooler weather
- lower humidity
- foliage
- fewer insects

Watch for:

- hunting traffic
- leaf-season crowds
- early freezing nights

# 13. Major Hazards

---

## 🐻 Black Bears

Use secure food storage.

Never leave accessible:

- food
- wrappers
- garbage
- cooking grease
- toothpaste
- scented toiletries

---

## 🕷️ Ticks / Lyme Disease

Pennsylvania is serious tick country.

Use:

- treated clothing
- repellents
- daily tick checks

---

## 🐍 Timber Rattlesnakes & Copperheads

Especially common in:

- rocky ridges
- south-facing slopes
- talus
- ledges
- warm rocky openings

---

## 🌊 Flash Flooding

Major concern in:

- river camps
- creek valleys
- island camps
- narrow hollows

Never assume a low gravel bar is safe just because it is dry.

---

## 🌳 Falling Trees / Limbs

Thunderstorms can create:

- widowmakers
- leaning trees
- hanging limbs
- blocked forest roads

Inspect overhead conditions before establishing camp.

---

## 🦌 Hunting Seasons

During hunting seasons:

- wear bright colors while hiking
- expect more vehicles
- avoid blocking hunter access
- do not camp directly beside heavily used hunting pullouts

---

## 🚚 Logging / Gas Operations

Working forest roads may have:

- logging trucks
- equipment
- active timber operations
- gas-development traffic

Yield and remain alert.

---

## ❄️ Snow / Black Ice

Especially dangerous on:

- shaded north-facing roads
- mountain grades
- narrow gravel roads
- bridges

---

## 📵 Long Zero-Signal Zones

Download before leaving town:

- offline maps
- MVUMs
- Forest Orders
- trail maps
- emergency information

# 14. Hyundai Accent Road-Safety Rules

A low-clearance Accent should turn around when encountering:

- deep standing water
- unknown creek crossings
- deep wheel ruts
- high center crowns
- exposed rock ledges
- fresh washouts
- soft clay/mud
- snow-covered steep grades
- large fallen trees
- badly eroded Level 2 roads

## Practical Rule

If the road ahead looks questionable:

1. park safely
2. walk the obstacle
3. check depth and clearance
4. confirm turnaround space
5. proceed only if boringly safe

The best recovery equipment is still:

> **not needing recovery.**

# Final Takeaway

Pennsylvania has **huge amounts of free primitive camping**, but its legal structure creates two very different systems.

## Best for Free Vehicle-Based Nomad Camping

**Allegheny National Forest**

Especially:

1. **FR160 / FR259**
2. **Sheffield-area Forest roads**
3. **Loleta Grade / FR131**
4. **Clarion River / Millstone Creek**
5. **Salmon Creek**

---

## Best for Free Backpack Camping

Pennsylvania's State Forest system.

Especially:

1. **Rothrock**
2. **Moshannon**
3. **Sproul**
4. **Tiadaghton**
5. **Susquehannock**
6. **Loyalsock**

---

## Most Important Rule

If you are using the Accent as an active part of the overnight camp on Pennsylvania State Forest land, the site is generally classified as **motorized roadside camping**, which is not free.

That means:

> **Pennsylvania is an Allegheny National Forest state for free car nomading, and a State Forest state for free backpacking.**
