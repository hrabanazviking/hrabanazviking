# Vermont Free Primitive Camping & Nomad Field Guide

**Prepared for low-cost vehicle-based nomad camping with a 2014 Hyundai Accent SE hatchback**

**Research status:** September 14, 2026

> **Important Vermont distinction:**  
> Vermont has several different public-land systems with different camping rules.  
> **Vermont state primitive camping is generally hike-in only**, while **Green Mountain National Forest (GMNF)** is the primary system for true free dispersed camping that can sometimes work with a vehicle-based nomad setup.

---

# 1. Vermont Camping Systems at a Glance

| Land System | General Free Camping? | Typical Stay Limit | Car-Adjacent Camping? |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Vermont FPR State Forest / State Park primitive areas | Yes | 3 consecutive nights in one area | **No** — generally hike at least 1,000 ft from a traveled road |
| Green Mountain National Forest dispersed camping | Yes | 14 days in any 30-day period | **Yes, in many legal dispersed roadside areas** |
| Vermont Wildlife Management Areas | Conditional | 3 consecutive nights | Not for ordinary nomad camping |

# 2. Important 2026 Regulatory Note

Vermont is actively revising its primitive-camping regulations.

As of **September 14, 2026**, the current rules remain in effect, but a proposed amendment is accepting public comments through **October 9, 2026**.

The proposed changes may include:

- Increasing campsite-to-water setbacks
- Tightening campfire requirements
- Revising primitive-camping management standards

Before camping later in 2026 or during 2027, recheck the current Vermont FPR rules.

Official resource:

https://fpr.vermont.gov/primitive-camping-rule-amendment

# 3. Vermont State-Land Primitive Camping Rules

On authorized Vermont Forests, Parks & Recreation primitive-camping lands, current rules generally require:

- Camp at least **100 feet from streams and water**
- Camp at least **200 feet from trails**
- Camp at least **200 feet from property boundaries**
- Camp at least **1,000 feet from a traveled road**
- Camp below **2,500 feet elevation** unless specifically designated otherwise
- Maximum stay of **3 consecutive nights in the same area**
- Groups of 11 or more generally require a permit
- Primitive sites do not provide:
  - Potable water
  - Toilets
  - Assigned sites
  - Campground services

## Critical vehicle-nomad limitation

Vermont state primitive camping is **not dispersed car camping**.

Your Hyundai Accent is primarily:

- Transportation to the trailhead or legal parking area
- A resupply vehicle
- A gear-storage base

You must hike away from the road to establish camp.

Official information:

https://fpr.vermont.gov/recreation/activities/camping/primitive-camping

# 4. Human Waste / Poop Disposal

When no privy is available:

1. Move at least **200 feet from water**
2. Dig a cathole at least **6 inches deep**
3. Deposit human waste
4. Cover completely with soil
5. Pack out:
   - Toilet paper where required or appropriate
   - Sanitary products
   - Wipes
   - Trash

Official primitive-camping guide:

https://www.vtstateparks.com/sites/stateparks/files/documents/camping-primitive.pdf

---

# 5. Campfire Rules

Vermont recommends using a self-contained camp stove whenever possible.

Where campfires are permitted:

- Use only dead and down wood
- Never cut live vegetation
- Keep fires small
- Never leave a fire unattended
- Extinguish completely
- Scatter or erase fire evidence when appropriate
- Follow temporary fire bans and emergency orders

Because rules may change under the proposed 2026 amendment, check local restrictions before every trip.

---

# 6. Wildlife Management Areas

Primitive camping on Vermont Wildlife Management Areas is **not general-purpose free camping**.

It is allowed only when associated with:

- Hunting
- Fishing
- Trapping

Typical WMA primitive-camping rules include:

- Maximum **3 consecutive nights**
- At least **200 feet from roads**
- At least **200 feet from property lines**
- At least **200 feet from water**

Do not use WMA camping as an ordinary nomad base unless you are legitimately engaged in a qualifying activity.

Official information:

https://vtfishandwildlife.com/hunt/find-a-place-to-hunt/find-a-wildlife-management-area/authorized-and-prohibited-activities-on-fw-lands/primitive-camping-on-wmas

# 10. Green Mountain National Forest Dispersed Camping

This is Vermont's most important system for a vehicle-based nomad.

General GMNF dispersed-camping guidance:

- Free dispersed camping is widely available unless closed
- Use existing campsites where possible
- Stay up to **14 days in any 30-day period**
- A roughly **200-foot separation from roads, water, streams, and trails** is strongly recommended when creating a new dispersed site
- Legal motor-vehicle access does **not** guarantee passenger-car suitability

Official dispersed camping information:

https://www.fs.usda.gov/Internet/FSE_MEDIA/stelprdb5318739.pdf

Motor Vehicle Use Map:

https://www.fs.usda.gov/sites/nfs/files/r09/gmfl/publication/GMNF_MVUM_2024.pdf

# 16. Seasonal Planning

## Winter — December through March

### Major concerns

- Deep snow
- Ice
- Locked gates
- Plow berms
- Unmaintained forest roads
- Frozen water
- Reduced solar production
- Severe cold at elevation

### Worst regions

- Northeast Kingdom
- Victory
- Willoughby
- High Green Mountains

### Accent strategy

Stay on:

- Paved roads
- Known plowed roads
- Low-elevation access points

Avoid unknown forest roads.

---

# 17. Mud Season — March through Late May

This is one of the worst periods for a low-clearance car.

Problems include:

- Saturated gravel
- Deep ruts
- Washouts
- Collapsed shoulders
- Closed gates
- Soft roadbeds
- Culvert failures

### Accent strategy

Favor:

- Austin Brook
- Pavement-adjacent camps
- Major-road corridors

Avoid:

- Deep seasonal forest roads
- Class IV roads
- Roads showing deep tire rutting

---

# 18. Late May through June

### Problems

- Black flies
- Mosquitoes
- Ticks
- Wet trails
- Remaining mud at elevation

Tick activity is especially important during late spring.

Carry:

- Tick remover
- Repellent
- Permethrin-treated clothing if desired
- Head net for black flies

---

# 19. July through September

Usually the best road-access period.

### Risks

- Thunderstorms
- Flash flooding
- High humidity
- Fallen limbs
- Sudden washouts after severe storms

Avoid camping in:

- Stream-bottom hollows
- Dry creek channels
- Narrow drainage bottoms

---

# 20. September through November

Often the best overall season.

Advantages:

- Fewer insects
- Cooler temperatures
- Firm roads
- Excellent scenery

Concerns:

- Hunting activity
- Early snow
- Cold nights
- Heavy tourist traffic during foliage season

Wear blaze orange when moving around public hunting lands.

# 21. Wildlife & Environmental Hazards

## Black Bears

Black bears are widespread across Vermont.

### Food safety

- Never store food in the tent
- Cook away from the sleeping area
- Secure food in a bear-resistant container where possible
- Never intentionally feed wildlife
- Secure trash
- Clean cookware thoroughly

---

## Ticks

Blacklegged ticks occur statewide.

Risk is particularly significant during:

- May-June
- Autumn activity periods

Check:

- Legs
- Waist
- Armpits
- Hairline
- Behind knees

- Deer and moose on rural roads
- Coyotes

- Severe thunderstorms
- Flooding
- Ice

- Black flies

- Mud

# 22. Hyundai Accent Rules for Vermont Forest Roads

Turn around when:

- Exposed rocks approach oil-pan height
- Ruts approach underbody depth
- Standing water hides the road surface
- Flowing water crosses the road
- The center crown begins scraping
- Mud reaches tire sidewalls
- You are unsure the car can climb back up a descent
- The road transitions into Class IV conditions
- Storm damage appears ahead

Your recovery equipment is valuable, but avoidance remains the safest recovery strategy.

# 30. Overall Verdict

Vermont is an excellent wilderness state, but it is **less convenient than New York for a vehicle-centered nomad**.

The key distinction is:

- **Vermont state forests:** primarily hike-in primitive camping
- **Green Mountain National Forest:** best source of actual dispersed car-accessible camping

For a 2014 Hyundai Accent, prioritize:

**Austin Brook → Kettle Brook → FR74 / VT 9 → Bingo Brook → Fay's Meadow → Mad Tom**

These areas offer the strongest overall combination of:

- Free camping
- Lower road risk
- Cellular internet
- Libraries
- Groceries
- Fuel
- Gyms
- Town access
- Low repeated-supply burden

Use deeper Green Mountain roads for short wilderness phases rather than as your primary operational base.
