# Executive Summary

Missouri is one of the stronger Midwestern / central-U.S. states for free primitive camping.

The state offers three especially useful systems:

1. **Mark Twain National Forest**
2. **Missouri Department of Conservation lands**
3. **Ozark National Scenic Riverways**

These systems operate under separate stay limits, which gives a slow nomad much more flexibility than a state where nearly all free camping falls under a single land manager.

Missouri Department of Conservation alone states that **more than 300 conservation areas offer free camping**. Not every area is equally useful for a vehicle-based nomad, and each conservation area may use different camping rules such as:

- designated sites only,
- individual primitive sites,
- open walk-in camping,
- or no camping in particular subareas.

This guide therefore emphasizes the **largest, most practical, and most useful free primitive-camping zones for a Hyundai Accent-based slow nomad**.

### Strongest overall Missouri choices

For the best blend of:

- legal free camping,
- easy Accent access,
- groceries,
- library,
- gym,
- fuel,
- internet,
- and low-cost resupply,

the strongest options are:

1. **Cedar Creek / Moon Loop**
2. **White Ranch Conservation Area**
3. **Indian Trail Conservation Area**
4. **Pine View / Piney Creek**
5. **Lead Mine Conservation Area**
6. **Willie Lee / Glade Top**

For maximum isolation:

- Rocky Creek Conservation Area
- Sunklands Conservation Area
- Irish Wilderness
- Hercules Glades Wilderness
- Peck Ranch Conservation Area

# Stay Limits

## Mark Twain National Forest

**Maximum: 14 days in any 30-day period across the entire National Forest.**

Moving from one Mark Twain district or wilderness to another does **not** restart the clock.

After reaching 14 camping days in the rolling 30-day period, camping equipment must be removed from National Forest land until enough days have passed.

---

## Missouri Department of Conservation

Current camping guidance:

- **14 consecutive days in a 30-day period**
- **30 total camping days per calendar year across all MDC lands**

Moving between MDC conservation areas does not create unlimited annual use.

MDC may have area-specific restrictions that are stricter.

---

## Ozark National Scenic Riverways Primitive Campgrounds

Typical primitive campground limit:

**14 days in a 30-day period at the same primitive campground.**

---

## Ozark National Scenic Riverways Gravel Bars

Maximum:

**5 days at one gravel bar.**

Gravel-bar camping is designed for people traveling by river.

# Human Waste / Poop Disposal

## Mark Twain Wilderness Areas

Typical wilderness guidance:

- Camp at least **100 feet from trails**
- Camp at least **100 feet from water**
- Camp at least **100 feet from other camps**
- Bury feces at least **6 inches deep**
- Completely cover the cathole
- Pack out wipes and trash

Packing out toilet paper is the cleanest option.

---

## Missouri Department of Conservation

Where no latrine is available:

- Go at least **200 feet from water**
- Go at least **200 feet from trails**
- Go at least **200 feet from campsites**
- Dig a hole approximately **6–8 inches deep**
- Completely cover it
- Do not leave visible toilet paper

---

## Ozark National Scenic Riverways

At primitive campgrounds / gravel bars:

- Bury waste at least **6 inches deep**
- Stay at least **100 feet from water**
- Stay at least **100 feet from trails**
- Stay at least **100 feet from developed facilities**

A portable toilet / WAG-style setup is cleaner in heavily used river corridors.

# Campfire Rules

## Mark Twain National Forest

General approach:

- Check temporary fire restrictions before arrival
- Keep fires small
- Use existing rings where practical
- Do not cut live trees
- Avoid damaging standing dead trees
- Completely extinguish fires before leaving
- Do not burn garbage

Some wilderness-specific instructions are stricter.

---

## Missouri Department of Conservation

Recommended:

- Keep fires small and controllable
- Clear nearby combustible material
- Keep water and a shovel nearby
- Never leave a fire unattended
- Use local firewood
- Avoid transporting ordinary firewood long distances

---

## Ozark National Scenic Riverways

Fires may be allowed unless current restrictions prohibit them.

Rules include:

- Burn wood completely to ash
- Drown and stir the fire
- Never burn garbage
- Never burn human waste
- Observe restrictions near cave mouths
- Observe restrictions near access points

---

# Vehicle and Road Rules

## Mark Twain National Forest

The **Motor Vehicle Use Map (MVUM)** is the legal reference for motorized travel.

Important:

- Stay on roads open to highway-legal vehicles
- Legal roads are not automatically passenger-car roads
- Forest roads may become rough, muddy, or washed out
- Creek crossings can rise rapidly

---

## Missouri Department of Conservation

Vehicle travel is generally limited to:

- paved roads,
- gravel roads,
- established public roads,
- and designated parking areas,

unless otherwise posted.

---

## Ozark National Scenic Riverways

NPS specifically warns that:

- many primitive campgrounds are remote,
- some approaches require high clearance,
- online navigation may be inaccurate,
- local maps should be used.

For a low-clearance Hyundai Accent, call the ranger station before attempting an unfamiliar primitive campground.

# Mark Twain National Forest Wilderness Areas

## 1. Bell Mountain Wilderness

### Character

Approx. 9,143 acres in the St. Francois Mountains.

Terrain includes:

- rocky rhyolite ridges
- steep slopes
- substantial local relief
- rugged hiking

### Accent Access

**3/5**

Primary trailheads are realistic in dry weather.

Avoid blindly exploring rough forest spurs.

### Supply Towns

- Ironton
- Arcadia
- Fredericktown
- Farmington

### Services

Moderate.

Larger towns provide:

- groceries
- library
- gym
- fuel
- auto services

### 📶 Nomad Connectivity Rating: 2/5

Expected:

- **T-Mobile:** weak
- **Verizon:** weak to moderate on exposed ridges
- **AT&T:** weak to moderate

### Terrain Obstruction

**High**

Ridges may work.

Hollows may be completely dead.

### Supply Pain

**3/5**

### Seasonal Notes

**Spring**
- wet
- rocky
- muddy
- slick

**Summer**
- hot
- humid
- ticks
- chiggers

**Fall**
- excellent
- hunting activity rises

**Winter**
- icy rock
- shaded road ice possible

### Hazards

- steep rock
- copperheads
- timber rattlesnakes
- ticks
- hunting activity
- limited water

# Gravel-Bar Camping

Free gravel-bar camping is available along portions of the:

- Current River
- Jacks Fork River

### Intended Use

Primarily for people traveling by:

- canoe
- kayak
- raft
- boat

Not intended as normal drive-up camping.

### Stay Limit

**5 days at one gravel bar**

### Spacing / Location Rules

Generally:

- 300 yards from developed / backcountry campgrounds
- at least 100 feet from another camping group
- at least 50 feet from landings / access points

### Nomad Use

Best treated as a side expedition rather than a Hyundai Accent base camp.

# Seasonal Strategy

## Spring: March-May

### Conditions

Spring is rough on low-clearance roads.

Expect:

- heavy rain
- mud
- creek crossings
- flash flooding
- thunderstorms
- severe weather
- soft gravel shoulders
- slippery clay

### Best Spring Accent Choices

- Cedar Creek / Moon Loop
- Indian Trail
- White Ranch
- Pine View
- Lead Mine main camping areas

### Avoid

Remote river-bottom sites and rough woods roads after heavy rain.

---

## Summer: June-August

### Conditions

Typical:

- highs near 90°F
- high humidity
- warm nights
- thunderstorms
- ticks
- chiggers
- mosquitoes
- poison ivy

### Strategy

Use:

- shaded camp
- ventilation
- insect protection
- daily tick checks

Avoid camping beneath dead limbs.

---

## Fall: September-Early November

Probably the best Missouri nomad window.

### Advantages

- cooler temperatures
- lower humidity
- beautiful Ozark forest
- firmer roads
- fewer bugs

### Concerns

- hunting season
- dry leaves
- wildfire risk
- shorter daylight

### Recommendation

Wear blaze orange during hunting seasons.

---

## Winter: December-February

Campable with proper gear.

Expect:

- freezing nights
- occasional snow
- freezing rain
- sleet
- freeze-thaw mud
- icy shaded roads

Missouri is better as a fall / spring transition state than a deep-winter base.

# Wildlife and Environmental Hazards

## Snakes

Possible species include:

- copperheads
- timber rattlesnakes
- cottonmouths in suitable wetland / river environments

---

## Black Bears

Missouri black bears occur in parts of the Ozarks.

Store food securely.

Do not leave food or scented trash around camp.

---

## Elk

Especially around:

- Peck Ranch
- nearby elk-restoration country

Give elk substantial space.

---

## Ticks

One of the biggest routine hazards.

Use:

- repellent
- treated clothing
- long pants
- daily tick checks

---

## Chiggers

Common in warm-season grass and brush.

Avoid direct contact with tall vegetation where possible.

---

## Poison Ivy

Extremely common.

Learn to identify it in multiple growth forms.

---

## Flash Flooding

Major risk in:

- river bottoms
- creek valleys
- gravel bars
- canyon-like hollows

Never camp in an obvious drainage channel.

---

## Severe Thunderstorms

Missouri storms can bring:

- lightning
- hail
- damaging wind
- flash floods
- tornadoes

Keep weather alerts available.

---

## Falling Limbs

Inspect overhead trees before choosing camp.

Avoid dead snags.

---

## Hunting

Many public lands are heavily hunted.

During seasons:

- wear blaze orange
- avoid camping directly beside heavily used hunting access
- know the current hunting calendar

# Final Nomad Verdict

Missouri is an **excellent slow-nomad camping state**.

Its strongest advantages are:

- large National Forest holdings
- hundreds of MDC areas
- free NPS primitive campgrounds
- river camping
- rugged Ozark wilderness
- enough town-adjacent options to keep resupply affordable
- several areas with workable cellular internet
- independent land-manager stay limits

Its biggest challenges are:

- rough gravel / forest roads
- river flooding
- flash floods
- weak cellular service in deep hollows
- ticks / chiggers
- hunting activity
- severe thunderstorms
- scattered services in the deepest Ozark zones

For a **2014 Hyundai Accent SE** and a nomad strategy emphasizing low fuel use, quiet nature, and practical internet, the top Missouri choices are:

**Cedar Creek / Moon Loop, White Ranch, Indian Trail, Pine View / Piney Creek, Lead Mine, and Willie Lee / Glade Top.**

For deep solitude:

**Rocky Creek, Sunklands, Irish Wilderness, Hercules Glades, and Peck Ranch** are the stronger wilderness-style choices.
