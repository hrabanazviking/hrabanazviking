# How North Dakota Free Camping Works

North Dakota has three especially important free-camping systems:

1. **Wildlife Management Areas**
2. **Federal dispersed public land**
3. **Free designated primitive campgrounds / backcountry areas**

North Dakota is unusual because most of its 200-plus state Wildlife Management Areas allow primitive camping by default, subject to local postings and specific exceptions.

## Public land does not always mean legal camping

Do not assume legal camping on:

- private ranch land
- PLOTS land without written landowner permission
- Waterfowl Production Areas
- state parks unless the campground is specifically free
- tribal land without tribal authorization
- closed WMA units
- roadsides or highway rights-of-way
- private roads leading to public parcels

## Essential offline information

Carry:

- current land-ownership maps
- Forest Service Motor Vehicle Use Maps
- WMA maps
- BLM parcel/access maps
- offline satellite imagery
- weather radar capability where signal exists
- local county road maps

# 1. North Dakota Wildlife Management Areas

## Why the WMA System Matters

North Dakota Game and Fish manages more than 200 Wildlife Management Areas.

Most allow primitive camping.

This creates a dense network of free camping opportunities across the state.

## Cost

**$0**

## Stay Limit

Maximum:

**10 consecutive days**

A tent, camper, vehicle, or camping equipment cannot simply be stored on a WMA.

The site must actually be used for lodging.

## Vehicle Rules

Motor vehicles are generally restricted to:

- constructed roads
- established trails
- parking areas used by passenger cars

Do not:

- drive cross-country
- create new campsite roads
- mow vegetation to make a site
- force a passenger car onto a rough route simply because it appears on a map

## Trash / Sewage

Waste must:

- leave with you
- or go into an authorized trash container

Dumping:

- camper holding tanks
- portable toilet tanks
- sewage
- wastewater

onto WMA land is prohibited.

## Human Waste

A statewide WMA rule specifically authorizing catholes was not found in the research.

### Safest approach

Use:

- portable toilet
- WAG bags
- pack-out sanitation system

This avoids uncertainty and repeated soil disturbance.

## Campfires

North Dakota Game and Fish allows gathering:

**dead/downed wood**

for campfires where fires are otherwise legal.

### Important firewood rule

Imported firewood is prohibited on WMAs.

Reason:

- emerald ash borer
- forest pest movement

Use local dead/down wood where legal or buy local wood.

Current fire restrictions always override ordinary WMA campfire permission.

- Maah Daah Hey Trail country

## Cost

**$0 dispersed camping**

## Stay Limit

- maximum **14 consecutive days at one site**
- maximum **28 cumulative camping days per calendar year**

**701-989-7300**

### Medora Ranger District
**701-227-7800**

### McKenzie Ranger District
**701-842-8500**

## Roads

Use:

- Motor Vehicle Use Maps
- legal public roads
- established Forest Service roads

## Accent Rule of Thumb

### Main gravel road suitable for passenger cars
**Yes**

### Dry decent dirt road
**Maybe; inspect first**

### Road marked unsuitable for passenger cars
**No**

### Wet badlands clay
**No**

The region contains:

- clay
- silt
- steep gullies
- deeply dissected badlands

## Accent Rating

**Moderate**

The Accent can access many useful sites.

Do not use a low-clearance front-wheel-drive car to chase the most remote possible site.

- pack toilet paper out

- WAG bags
- pack-out system

## Campfires

- Forest Service alerts

- heat spikes

### September-Early October

## Cost

**$0 backcountry camping**

## Stay Limit

Maximum:

**14 days**

**24 hours**

## Human Waste

Because a statewide State Forest rule explicitly authorizing catholes was not found:

# Places Commonly Mistaken for Free Primitive Camping

## PLOTS Land

PLOTS:

**Private Land Open To Sportsmen**

Camping requires:

**written landowner permission**

Do not treat PLOTS as public camping land.

## USFWS Waterfowl Production Areas

Federal Waterfowl Production Areas often prohibit:

- camping
- open fires
- overnight parking

Do not confuse them with state WMAs.

## North Dakota State Parks

Primitive campsites are generally:

**not free**

Example:

Little Missouri State Park charges for primitive camping.

## Developed Dakota Prairie Grasslands Campgrounds

Most developed Forest Service campgrounds charge fees.

The free option is usually:

**dispersed camping**

unless the specific campground is explicitly $0.

# Fire Rules Statewide

North Dakota fire conditions can change extremely quickly.

## Before every fire

Check:

1. NDResponse fire-danger map
2. county restrictions
3. Red Flag Warnings
4. land-manager alerts
5. local posted rules
6. current wind

## Best Nomad Strategy

Use a:

**propane or liquid-fuel stove as your primary cooking system**

Treat campfire as optional.

This prevents fire restrictions from disrupting your food system.

## Firewood

Because of emerald ash borer:

- do not move firewood long distances
- do not bring outside firewood into WMAs
- buy local
- gather local dead/down wood only where permitted

# North Dakota Hazard Guide

## 1. Hail

North Dakota can produce severe hail.

Threats include:

- golf-ball hail
- larger hail
- straight-line wind

A hard-top vehicle gives better storm protection than a tent.

Know the nearest:

- town
- fuel stop
- solid building
- public shelter

## 2. Lightning

Open prairie offers little protection.

Avoid:

- hilltops
- lone trees
- ridge tops
- exposed metal fencing

Use a hard-top car for severe electrical storms when possible.

## 3. Gumbo Mud

Western North Dakota clay is one of the biggest vehicle hazards.

A road can change from:

**firm → greasy → impossible**

very quickly.

If meaningful rain is approaching:

**return to reliable gravel before the storm.**

## 4. Wildfire

Especially important in:

- Little Missouri
- BLM land
- dry WMAs
- grasslands

Carry:

- ABC fire extinguisher
- shovel
- gas stove

## 5. High Wind

North Dakota wind can:

- collapse tents
- damage awnings
- intensify wildfire
- create dangerous winter wind chill
- make prairie camping exhausting

Use:

- strong stakes
- low-profile shelter
- sheltered camps where legal

## 6. Winter

Expect:

- subzero temperatures
- blizzards
- whiteouts
- drifting snow
- ice
- road closures
- frozen water systems

Winter primitive camping requires a fundamentally different setup.

## 7. Rattlesnakes

Mainly western badlands.

Watch:

- rocky slopes
- prairie-dog towns
- warm road edges
- brush piles

Use a flashlight at night.

## 8. Ticks

Important statewide, especially in:

- forests
- eastern grassland
- wet vegetation

Perform frequent tick checks.

## 9. Mosquitoes

Especially heavy in:

- Turtle Mountains
- Sheyenne
- river areas
- wetlands
- eastern North Dakota

## 10. Prairie-Dog Colonies

Hazards include:

- deep holes
- rough vehicle terrain
- rattlesnakes
- plague around dead rodents

Never handle dead prairie dogs.

## 11. Hunting Season

Many WMAs and public lands become busy in fall.

Recommended:

- blaze-orange outer layer
- visible camp
- avoid active shooting corridors
- avoid parking in obvious hunting pullouts overnight when possible

## 12. Oilfield Traffic

Western North Dakota roads can have:

- heavy trucks
- dust
- fast industrial traffic
- wide loads

Be particularly cautious near:

- Watford City
- Williston
- McKenzie County
- Dunn County

## 13. Private-Land Checkerboarding

Especially important on:

- BLM parcels
- grasslands
- western badlands

Never assume:

**public parcel = public route to parcel**

Confirm legal access.

# Final Rule

When choosing a free North Dakota campsite, think in this order:

**land ownership → local camping rule → legal access → rain/mud risk → weather/hail → fire restriction → water → sanitation → stay limit → resupply**

For a low-clearance front-wheel-drive car, one extra rule belongs near the top:

**If western North Dakota clay is wet, do not gamble on the road.**
