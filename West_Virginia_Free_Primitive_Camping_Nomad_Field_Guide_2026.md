# 1. What Counts as Free Primitive Camping in West Virginia

The major systems for legitimate free primitive or dispersed camping are:

- **Monongahela National Forest**
- **George Washington & Jefferson National Forests**
- **New River Gorge National Park & Preserve**
- **Gauley River National Recreation Area**

## Important exclusions

### West Virginia Wildlife Management Areas

West Virginia Wildlife Management Areas are **not general free-dispersed-camping land**. Camping is normally restricted to designated areas, and WVDNR campground fees may apply.

### State fishing and boating access areas

These should **not** be treated as overnight camping sites unless specifically posted otherwise.

### Older "free campground" lists

Several older boondocking websites are outdated.

Beginning in **2026**, Monongahela National Forest introduced or increased fees at several developed sites that older sources may still label free. These include locations associated with:

- Bear Heaven
- Cranberry
- Williams River
- Summit Lake
- Laurel Fork
- Stonecoal
- Day Run
- Tea Creek
- Red Creek
- Developed Lake Sherwood campground

This guide separates **true free dispersed/primitive camping** from developed campgrounds that now charge fees.

# 3. Monongahela National Forest

Monongahela National Forest contains the largest network of free dispersed camping in West Virginia.

## General dispersed-camping stay rule

A common Forest Service rule is:

- **14 cumulative days within a 28-day period at one campsite**
- Move when the allowed occupancy period ends
- Special areas may have stricter rules or designated-site-only restrictions

Always verify the current Forest Order for the exact ranger district.

---

## Human Waste

For true backcountry/dispersed camping:

- Dig a cathole approximately **6–8 inches deep**
- Stay at least **200 feet from water**
- Stay well away from roads, campsites, and trails
- Pack toilet paper out
- A portable toilet/WAG-bag system is preferable for vehicle camping

---

## Campfires

Where fires are permitted:

- Prefer existing fire rings
- Use dead-and-down wood only
- Keep fires small
- Do not cut live vegetation
- Completely extinguish the fire before leaving
- Follow current fire restrictions and local Forest Orders

---

## Bear Food Storage

Southern Monongahela districts have had food-storage requirements because of black bear activity.

Keep:

- food
- garbage
- pet food
- toothpaste
- scented toiletries

secured according to the current district order.

# 6. New River Gorge National Park & Preserve

New River Gorge National Park & Preserve currently operates **eight free primitive campgrounds**.

All are:

- free
- first come, first served
- primitive
- no electrical hookups
- no potable-water system

Some have toilets.

## Stay Limit

A common NPS rule is:

**14 total days within a 28-day period across park camping areas.**

---

## Human Waste

Where a campground toilet exists:

**Use it.**

For legal backcountry camping well away from a toilet:

- carry waste out, or
- bury waste about **6 inches deep**
- stay at least **200 feet from water**
- stay out of sight of campsites/trails/roads where possible

---

## Campfires

Where permitted:

- use existing fire receptacles/rings
- dead-and-down wood only
- fully extinguish
- obey temporary fire restrictions

## 7.3 Gauley Backcountry Camping

Backcountry camping can be legal on **federally owned land** within Gauley River NRA.

# 8. Seasonal Conditions

### High Monongahela

Usually the worst season for Accent-based dispersed camping.

Expect:

- snow
- ice
- unplowed roads
- frozen ruts
- seasonal gates
- cold nights
- downed trees

### New River / Gauley

Much more viable.

Remaining risks:

- shaded-road ice
- flooding
- winter storms
- steep icy secondary roads

# 9. Major Hazards

West Virginia's main camping hazards include:

## 🐻 Black Bears

Store food and scented items securely.

Never leave:

- food
- wrappers
- cooking grease
- toothpaste
- scented toiletries

accessible around camp.

---

## 🌊 Flash Flooding

Especially important in:

- river valleys
- creek corridors
- New River Gorge
- Gauley River
- Gandy Creek
- Little River

Never camp directly in:

- dry wash channels
- low gravel bars
- obviously flood-scoured ground

---

## 🌳 Falling Limbs

After storms, Appalachian forests can produce:

- widowmakers
- leaning trees
- broken limbs
- road-blocking trees

Inspect overhead hazards before setting camp.

---

## 🕷️ Ticks

Treat clothing/gear appropriately and perform regular tick checks.

---

## 🐍 Venomous Snakes

West Virginia has venomous snake habitat.

Watch:

- rock piles
- sunny ledges
- wood piles
- creek banks

---

## 🦌 Hunting Seasons

During hunting seasons:

- wear visible colors away from camp
- avoid setting camp directly beside heavily used hunting pullouts
- expect more vehicle traffic

---

## 🌫️ Fog

Mountain fog can severely reduce road visibility.

Avoid driving unfamiliar gravel roads at night or in dense fog.

---

## 🚂 Railroad Hazards

New River Gorge has active rail corridors.

Never use:

- tracks
- railroad bridges
- railroad tunnels
- railroad right-of-way

as trails.

---

## 💣 Dolly Sods Ordnance

Never touch suspicious military objects or ammunition-like debris.

Leave the area and report it if necessary.

---

# 10. Hyundai Accent Road-Safety Rules

Traction boards, a tow rope, recovery gear, and careful driving help.

They do **not** change the vehicle's ground clearance.

Turn around when encountering:

- deep standing water
- an unobservable water crossing
- high center ridges
- wheel ruts deeper than the Accent's clearance
- fresh washouts
- mud already packing tire tread
- loose steep grades
- fallen trees
- large rock shelves

## Practical Rule

If you are thinking:

> "I can probably make that."

Stop and inspect it on foot first.

The cheapest recovery is the one you never need.

# Best Strategy for a Slow Nomad in a Hyundai Accent

A practical West Virginia rotation would look something like:

1. **Gauley Tailwaters**
   - Use as a civilization-friendly base
   - Internet, groceries, library and gym nearby

2. **War Ridge / Grandview area**
   - Stay near Beckley while exploring New River Gorge

3. **Lake Sherwood / Anthony Creek**
   - Move into Monongahela while maintaining access to Lewisburg

4. **Canaan / Davis region**
   - Use easy-access camps only

5. **Lower Glady / Mower / Gandy**
   - Schedule these as intentional offline retreat periods

This reduces fuel use while still preserving long stretches of deep forest solitude.

# Final Takeaway

West Virginia is excellent for a slow nomad who wants **deep forest solitude**, but the best locations divide into two very different classes.

## Practical Connected Bases

Best for normal nomad life:

1. **Gauley Tailwaters**
2. **War Ridge**
3. **Lake Sherwood dispersed corridor**
4. **Grandview Sandbar**
5. **Canaan Loop eastern sites**

## Deep Offline Retreats

Best when you deliberately want isolation:

1. **Lower Glady**
2. **Gandy Creek**
3. **Mower Basin**
4. **Little River**
5. **Middle Mountain**
6. **Dolly Sods**

The standout location is **Gauley Tailwaters** because it combines nearly everything that is usually mutually exclusive in Appalachian free camping:

- legal free camping
- low-clearance-car access
- nearby supplies
- nearby town services
- relatively painless repeated drives
- a realistic chance of useful cellular internet

That makes it the strongest all-around West Virginia base for a Hyundai Accent nomad setup.
