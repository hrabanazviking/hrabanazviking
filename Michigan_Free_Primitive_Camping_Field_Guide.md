# How Michigan Free Primitive Camping Works

Michigan's free primitive-camping system is a dense mosaic of:

- Michigan DNR State Forest land
- Huron-Manistee National Forests
- Hiawatha National Forest
- Ottawa National Forest
- federal wilderness areas
- selected county forest systems

The main vehicle hazards are usually **deep sugar sand, spring mud, standing water, flooded two-tracks, hidden potholes, seasonal snow, downed trees, logging activity, and ORV trails mistaken for roads**.

---

# 1. Michigan DNR State Forest Dispersed Camping

## Cost
**$0**

## Registration
A **free dispersed camping registration card** must be completed and posted at the campsite.

## Location Restrictions
The campsite must generally:
- be on legal State Forest land
- be outside a state park or recreation area
- be outside a developed campground
- not be posted closed
- not be in a State Game Area under the ordinary public-facing dispersed camping rule
- be more than one mile from a State Forest campground

## Stay Limit
- **15 consecutive nights** normally
- **up to 21 days from October 1 through May 1**

After reaching the limit, move at least **1 mile from the previous campsite**.

The camp cannot be left unoccupied for more than **24 hours**.

## Vehicle Placement
A vehicle should generally remain within **50 feet of the traveled portion of a legal road, forest road, parking area, or trail open to that vehicle**.

## Road Network
Michigan maintains roughly **13,494 miles of inventoried State Forest roads**. Roads are not guaranteed to remain open, dry, plowed, or passable.

## Human Waste
A statewide DNR rule expressly authorizing catholes was not found in the research pass.

**Safest system:** portable toilet, WAG bags, or another legal pack-out system.

## Campfires
Use an existing ring where available or mineral soil, keep water nearby, and fully extinguish the fire.

## Firewood
Immediate personal campfire fuel gathered while legally dispersed camping can be covered by the State Forest dispersed-camping registration framework. Avoid moving firewood long distances.

# 2. Huron-Manistee National Forests

## Cost
**$0 dispersed camping**

## Permit
**No ordinary dispersed-camping permit required**

## Stay Limit
**16 days at one location within a 30-day period**

## Campsite Placement
Typical guidance:
- at least 200 feet from water
- roughly 150 feet from roads/trails under forest planning guidance
- use existing disturbed sites where possible

## Vehicle Access
Use the current **Motor Vehicle Use Map**.

Do not confuse a forest road with an ORV or motorcycle trail.

## Accent Rating
**Good to moderate**

This is one of Michigan's strongest federal systems for a Hyundai Accent.

## Supply
**West:** Baldwin, White Cloud, Manistee, Cadillac  
**East:** Mio, Oscoda, Tawas, West Branch

## Contacts
- Supervisor, Cadillac: **231-775-2421**
- Baldwin / White Cloud: **231-745-4631**
- Cadillac / Manistee: **231-723-2211**
- Mio: **989-826-3252**
- Huron Shores / Oscoda: **989-739-0728**

## Sanitation
Where locally legal and no toilet exists:
- 6-8 inch cathole
- about 200 feet from water, camp and trail
- pack toilet paper out

A portable toilet is easier for vehicle camping.

## Fire
Local forest wood may generally be gathered for campsite use where legal. Check active restrictions before burning.

# 3. Nordhouse Dunes Wilderness

## Cost
**$0 wilderness camping**

Nearby developed parking/day-use facilities may charge separate recreation fees.

## Access
No motorized travel inside the wilderness.

**Accent: excellent to legal parking, then hike.**

## Camping Distance Rules
- **400 feet** from Lake Michigan / wilderness boundary
- **200 feet** from Nordhouse Lake
- **100 feet** from established trails

## Group Size
About **10 people maximum**.

## Fire
Campfires must remain:
- 400 feet from Lake Michigan
- 200 feet from Nordhouse Lake

Do not burn dune wood, driftwood, pine knots or shipwreck timber.

## Supply
Ludington, Manistee, Baldwin.

## Best Season
Late May through October, with **September especially strong**.

# Places Commonly Mistaken for Free Primitive Camping

## Michigan State Forest Campgrounds
Rustic, but **not free**. Typical current rates are around **$20/night**, and Recreation Passport requirements can also apply.

## Pictured Rocks National Lakeshore
Not dispersed-free. Camping requires designated campsites, reservations and fees. Sleeping in parking lots or camping outside authorized campgrounds is prohibited.

## Commercial Forest Program Land
Private forest land may be open for hunting, fishing and trapping access, but **camping is not included without landowner permission**.

## Sylvania Wilderness
Uses its own designated-site and permit system. Do not treat it like ordinary free Ottawa dispersed camping.

## Au Sable Special Guidance
Official primitive-camping guidance recommends:
- portable toilet

or, where appropriate:
- hole 6-12 inches deep
- high ground
- at least 100 feet from water

## Never
- dump portable toilet waste on the ground
- dump RV tanks
- bury plastic bags
- leave exposed toilet paper
- contaminate lakes or streams

# Campfires and Firewood

Use **propane as the primary cooking system** and treat wood fire as optional.

Before every fire, check:
- Michigan DNR fire alert
- Forest Service restrictions
- county rules
- wind
- Red Flag Warnings
- local posting

Hiawatha and Ottawa prohibit importing firewood from outside the Upper Peninsula.

# Black Bear and Food Storage

Michigan has **black bears**, not grizzlies.

Keep out of the tent:
- food
- trash
- toiletries
- coolers
- grease
- pet food
- scented products

A closed hard-sided vehicle is useful for food storage.

Michigan DNR recommends bear spray as a precaution in bear country.

# The Michigan Sugar-Sand Rule

Michigan's deceptively dangerous vehicle surface is **deep dry sand**.

If the front tires begin:
- plowing
- spinning
- digging
- losing steering response

reverse out early.

Do not continue until the underbody settles into the road.

---

# ORV Trail Warning

Never confuse an **ORV trail** with a **forest road**.

Some Michigan ORV trails are only:
- 40 inches wide
- 50 inches wide
- heavily whooped
- sandy
- rutted

They are not normal-car roads.

# Michigan Hazard Guide

## Deep Sugar Sand
Major vehicle hazard in northern Lower Michigan and sandy forest routes.

## Spring Mud
Freeze/thaw and snowmelt can make roads soft, rutted and flooded.

## Lake-Effect Snow
Major risk in the western UP, Marquette, Munising, Gaylord and Lake Michigan snowbelts.

## Black Bears
Secure food and trash.

## Ticks
Blacklegged ticks can transmit Lyme disease. Tick activity matters roughly March through November.

## Mosquitoes
Heavy in wetlands and northern forests.

## Black Flies
Especially intense in late spring / early summer.

## Wildfire
Dry jack pine, grass and forest litter can burn rapidly.

## Smoke
Canadian wildfire smoke can severely reduce air quality.

## Deadfall
Avoid camping under dead standing trees or hanging limbs.

## Logging Trucks
Drive slowly and stay right on working forest roads.

## ORV Traffic
Expect ATVs, side-by-sides and motorcycles.

## Hunting Season
Wear blaze orange or another highly visible outer layer when walking in fall.

## Great Lakes Shore Weather
Wind, fog, rapid cooling, rain and snow can differ sharply from nearby inland conditions.

# Recommended Slow-Nomad Rotation

## Late Spring
Start with:
- Gladwin
- Roscommon
- lower Huron-Manistee
- Cadillac

Move farther north as roads dry.

## Summer
Best choices:
- Huron-Manistee
- northern Lower State Forest
- Hiawatha
- eastern/central UP State Forest

## September
Probably the strongest statewide month.

Excellent:
- Cadillac
- Grayling
- Pigeon River
- Traverse/Kalkaska
- Hiawatha
- Ottawa
- Marquette County Forest
- Delta County Forest
- Nordhouse Dunes

## October
Favor:
- Lower Peninsula
- southern/central State Forest
- Huron-Manistee

Monitor UP snow carefully.

## Winter
Remote Michigan forest camping becomes a different operating mode because of:
- lake-effect snow
- buried roads
- freezing rain
- frozen water
- weak solar
- gates
- inaccessible sites

# Final Rule

When choosing a Michigan free primitive campsite, think in this order:

**land ownership → camping legality → road type → sand/mud risk → weather → fire status → sanitation → stay limit → supply town**

For a Hyundai Accent, add two rules:

**Never assume a flat sandy road is easy just because it is dry.**

**Never confuse an ORV trail with a forest road.**
