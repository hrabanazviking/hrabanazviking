# Executive Summary

Illinois has far fewer free primitive-camping options than western public-land states.

The overwhelming majority of genuinely free legal primitive camping is concentrated in **Shawnee National Forest** in southern Illinois.

There are two main forms:

- **Dispersed / backcountry camping** on appropriate National Forest land.
- A small number of **free primitive campground or openland areas** such as:
  - Turkey Bayou
  - Tower Rock
  - Pennant Bar Openlands
  - Ashby Openlands

Most Illinois state-park primitive campsites are **fee-based**, so they are not included here as free nomad camping.

For a low-clearance **2014 Hyundai Accent SE**, the best practical balance of road access, supplies, solitude, and cellular service is generally found around:

1. **Dutchman Lake**
2. **Kinkaid Lake**
3. **Cedar Lake**
4. **Bald Knob Wilderness**
5. **Garden of the Gods**

For deeper isolation and weaker cellular service:

- Lusk Creek Wilderness
- Bay Creek Wilderness
- Burden Falls Wilderness
- Jackson Falls

# Important Shawnee National Forest Stay Limit

Current Shawnee National Forest rules impose a forest-wide occupancy limit:

> **Maximum stay: 14 days during any 28-day period, with no more than 28 cumulative camping days anywhere within Shawnee National Forest during a calendar year.**

Moving to another camping area inside Shawnee **does not reset the clock**.

This means Shawnee works well as:

- one 14-day stay plus another 14-day stay later, or
- two different 14-day zones during the year.

It does **not** work as a months-long rotating home base.

Additional forest-wide restrictions include:

- No camping within **1/4 mile of a developed campground or picnic ground**
- No camping within **150 feet of Cedar Lake**
- No camping within **150 feet of Little Cedar Lake**
- No camping within **150 feet of Kinkaid Lake**
- No camping in officially closed areas

# General Primitive Camping Rules

Unless a local order is stricter:

- Camp only on land where dispersed camping is legal.
- Confirm that you are on **National Forest land**, especially around lakes where ownership can be patchy.
- Avoid creating new heavily impacted campsites when an existing site is available.
- Keep camps away from trails and water.
- Pack out all garbage.
- Do not burn trash.
- Follow posted closure orders.
- Respect wilderness restrictions.
- Avoid damaging vegetation.
- Do not block roads, gates, trailheads, or emergency access.

---

# Human Waste / Poop Disposal

For backcountry camping:

- Dig a cathole approximately **6 to 8 inches deep**.
- Stay at least **200 feet from water**.
- Stay at least **200 feet from trails**.
- Stay at least **200 feet from campsites** when possible.
- Cover the cathole completely after use.
- Pack out wipes and hygiene products.
- Packing out toilet paper is the cleanest option and is recommended in many Forest Service Leave No Trace guidelines.

For a vehicle-based nomad setup, a **portable toilet + waste bags / gel system** may be cleaner and easier than digging in wet, rocky, or heavily used locations.

---

# Campfire Rules

General guidance:

- Use an existing fire ring when possible.
- Burn only **dead and down wood** where gathering is legal.
- Never cut live trees.
- Keep fires small.
- Never burn garbage.
- Fully extinguish the fire before leaving.
- Avoid fires beneath cliffs or near rock shelters.
- Observe temporary drought and wildfire restrictions.

Some Shawnee restrictions prohibit campfires within approximately **100 feet of cliff bases and rock shelters**.

Always check the current Forest Service fire restrictions immediately before camping.

# Vehicle and Road Rules

Use only roads legally open to motor vehicles.

The **Motor Vehicle Use Map (MVUM)** is the legal reference for Forest Service motorized travel.

Important:

- A road being legally open does **not** mean it is safe for a Hyundai Accent.
- Gravel roads are usually manageable.
- Dirt roads may become impassable after rain.
- Low-water crossings can become dangerous quickly.
- Openlands can become extremely soft and muddy.
- Avoid following trucks or SUVs blindly onto marginal roads.

### Hyundai Accent practical rule

If the road has:

- deep ruts,
- loose rock larger than a fist,
- visible mud,
- standing water,
- steep washouts,
- a crown high enough to scrape the underbody,

turn around.

# Individual Camping Areas

**Best all-around Illinois option for a Hyundai Accent.**

Dutchman Lake provides a strong balance of:

- good road access,
- free dispersed camping,
- relatively easy resupply,
- proximity to Vienna,
- lower terrain difficulty,
- a reasonable chance of cellular service.

### Access

Generally passenger-car friendly.

**Accent score:** 5/5

### Nearest supply town

**Vienna**

Useful for:

- groceries
- fuel
- basic supplies
- town services

### Supply pain

**1/5**

One of the easiest Shawnee areas for repeat supply runs.

### 📶 Nomad Connectivity Rating: 3/5

Expected:

- **T-Mobile:** possible
- **Verizon:** some LTE / variable
- **AT&T:** weaker / inconsistent

- chiggers

## 2. Kinkaid Lake Backcountry

### Important ownership warning

Kinkaid Lake is surrounded by mixed land ownership.

Only camp on land where **National Forest dispersed camping is legal**.

Do not assume all undeveloped shoreline is Forest Service land.

### Shoreline restriction

Camp at least **150 feet from Kinkaid Lake**.

### Access

Main approaches are generally manageable.

Some spurs are much rougher.

**Accent score:** 4/5

### Current road issue

**McClure Road / FSR 772** is currently closed at a failed low-water crossing / culvert.

Avoid planning access through that section until reopened.

### Supply towns

- **Murphysboro**
- **Carbondale**

These offer some of the strongest supply infrastructure near free Shawnee camping.

### Supply pain

**1/5**

Excellent for repeated nomad resupply.

### 📶 Nomad Connectivity Rating: 3/5

Expected:

- **T-Mobile:** some coverage
- **Verizon:** some coverage
- **AT&T:** often promising closer to populated corridors

Deep coves and wooded terrain can reduce signal rapidly.

### Internet dependability

Moderately promising near outer portions of the lake and road corridors.

# Seasonal Strategy

## Spring: March-May

### Conditions

Spring is one of the most difficult seasons for low-clearance vehicle access.

Expect:

- frequent rain
- muddy dirt roads
- swollen creeks
- slick sandstone
- soft openlands
- low-water crossings becoming dangerous

### Best Accent choices

- Dutchman Lake
- Garden of the Gods main access
- Kinkaid main access
- Cedar Lake main access

### Avoid

Marginal openland and remote dirt-road approaches immediately after prolonged rain.

---

## Summer: June-August

### Conditions

Typical hazards:

- upper-80s°F daytime heat
- high humidity
- warm nights
- thunderstorms
- flash flooding
- mosquitoes
- ticks
- chiggers
- poison ivy
- falling branches

### Strategy

Favor shaded areas but maintain good airflow.

Avoid parking or camping beneath dead trees.

Monitor thunderstorm forecasts.

---

## Fall: September-November

Probably the best Illinois nomad season.

### Advantages

- cooler temperatures
- lower humidity
- beautiful forest
- generally comfortable camping

### Concerns

- hunting season
- dry leaf litter
- wildfire risk
- shorter daylight later in season

### Recommendation

Wear visible orange when hiking during hunting seasons.

---

## Winter: December-February

Possible, but not ideal for a winter nomad route compared with Tennessee or Mississippi.

### Expect

- freezing nights
- occasional snow
- ice
- slick sandstone
- short daylight
- freeze-thaw mud

Southern Illinois is warmer than northern Illinois but is still a true winter climate.

# Wildlife and Environmental Hazards

## Snakes

Southern Illinois supports:

- copperheads
- timber rattlesnakes
- cottonmouths in suitable wetland / river-bottom habitats

Be especially alert around:

- rock ledges
- downed timber
- warm trail edges
- creek bottoms
- wetlands

---

## Ticks

Ticks are one of the most important everyday hazards.

Use:

- treated clothing
- frequent body checks
- long pants
- insect repellent where appropriate

---

## Chiggers

Very common in warm grassy / brushy areas.

Avoid sitting directly in vegetation.

---

## Poison Ivy

Extremely common.

Learn to identify it in multiple growth forms.

---

## Flash Flooding

Major concern in:

- creek bottoms
- canyon areas
- river bottomlands
- Turkey Bayou
- Tower Rock
- low-water crossings

Never camp directly in a drainage channel.

---

## Sandstone Cliffs

Common at:

- Garden of the Gods
- Jackson Falls
- Lusk Creek
- Burden Falls
- Clear Springs

Wet sandstone can become extremely slippery.

---

## Falling Limbs

Thunderstorms and dead trees can drop branches without warning.

Inspect the canopy before selecting a campsite.

## Final Nomad Verdict

Illinois is not a giant public-land camping state, but **Shawnee National Forest is absolutely worth using as part of a national nomad route**.

Its strongest advantages are:

- beautiful sandstone wilderness
- numerous dispersed camping opportunities
- several free primitive camping zones
- relatively short distances to towns
- mild fall weather
- excellent hiking
- enough road-accessible options for a Hyundai Accent

Its biggest limitations are:

- strict **28-days-per-calendar-year total**
- muddy roads after rain
- weak cellular service in canyon country
- ticks / chiggers / poison ivy
- flooding in bottomlands
- patchwork land ownership around some lakes

For a Hyundai Accent-based nomad who wants both solitude and workable logistics, **Dutchman Lake, Kinkaid Lake, Cedar Lake, Bald Knob, and Garden of the Gods** are the strongest Illinois choices.
