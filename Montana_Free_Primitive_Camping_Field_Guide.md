## Dispersed Public-Land Camping, Free Primitive Campgrounds, Rules, Roads, Bears, Fire, Sanitation, Seasonal Access, Supply Towns, and Hyundai Accent Suitability

**Research status:** September 11, 2026  
**Scope:** Legal no-camping-fee primitive camping in Montana, including dispersed National Forest and BLM camping, selected free Forest Service and BLM campgrounds, Charles M. Russell National Wildlife Refuge, Upper Missouri River Breaks land-based dispersed camping, and selected Montana state lands that have no nightly camping fee but may require a Conservation License.

# How Montana Free Primitive Camping Works

Montana has one of the largest legal free-camping systems in the United States.

The main camping layers are:

1. **National Forest dispersed camping**
2. **BLM dispersed camping**
3. **free named Forest Service campgrounds**
4. **free named BLM campgrounds/recreation sites**
5. **Charles M. Russell National Wildlife Refuge**
6. **Upper Missouri River Breaks land-based public camping**
7. **State Trust Land with Conservation License**
8. **FWP Wildlife Management Areas with Conservation License**

## A campsite is not always a named campsite

Much of Montana's free camping is area-based.

You may be camping:

- beside a legal Forest Service road
- on a legal BLM spur
- at an existing primitive clearing
- on refuge land near a numbered road
- at an undeveloped recreation site

There is no finite list of every legal dispersed campsite.

## Essential rule

Public land does not automatically mean:

- legal vehicle access
- legal overnight camping
- legal campfire
- legal food storage
- legal human-waste burial
- legal road use

Always verify the land manager and current local order.

# General National Forest Rules

Montana's National Forests fall under the Forest Service Northern Region camping occupancy order.

## Stay Limit

Current regional rule:

- **maximum 16 consecutive days at one site**
- **maximum 32 cumulative camping days during a calendar year**

A campsite generally includes the surrounding half-mile area for occupancy purposes.

Some local districts or special corridors may impose:

- shorter stays
- seasonal limits
- different distance requirements
- food-storage rules
- river-corridor waste rules
- vehicle restrictions

## Vehicle Access

Use the current:

**Motor Vehicle Use Map (MVUM)**

Do not assume that:

- an old logging road is legal
- a visible two-track is legal
- a map line is open
- a gate being open means overnight motorized access is permitted

## Best Practice

Prefer:

- existing campsites
- already disturbed surfaces
- legal roads
- existing fire rings

Avoid creating new vehicle tracks.

## Human Waste

The safest conservative Forest Service method is:

- 6-8 inch cathole
- roughly 200 feet from water
- away from trails
- away from camps
- pack toilet paper out

Where local rules require pack-out, follow the local rule instead.

For vehicle camping, a portable toilet or WAG system is often superior.

## Fire

When unrestricted:

- use existing rings
- use dead/down wood where legal
- never cut standing live timber
- fully extinguish all fires

Local fire restrictions override ordinary rules.

## Bears

Many western Montana forests have mandatory food-storage orders.

Store:

- food
- trash
- toiletries
- cookware
- coolers
- pet food
- scented items

in an approved bear-resistant manner.

A closed hard-sided vehicle may qualify in many areas when windows are fully closed, but check the local order.

# General BLM Dispersed Camping

Montana contains very large areas of BLM public land.

## Cost

**$0 dispersed camping**

unless a developed site charges a fee.

## Stay Limit

Typical Montana-Dakotas BLM rule:

**16 days**

Some specific recreation areas impose shorter limits.

## Legal Access

A public parcel does not create legal access across private ranch land.

Verify:

- public county road
- BLM road
- easement
- legal adjoining public land

## Human Waste

BLM currently favors:

- portable toilets
- pack-out waste systems

Where burial is permitted:

- 6-8 inch cathole
- at least 200 feet from water
- at least 200 feet from trails

## Accent Rating

Highly variable.

Good on:

- maintained gravel
- known BLM access roads

Poor on:

- wet clay
- deeply rutted roads
- 4WD routes
- unmaintained spurs

**Conservation License**

**16 days in 30**

**16 days in 30**

**50 feet of the customary access point**

## Tent / Nonmotorized Camping

Generally within approximately:

**200 feet of the customary access point**

**Campfires prohibited**

- public road
- adjoining public land
- legal water access
- landowner permission
- established public access agreement

Usually **good**, because legal camping is generally tied closely to established access points rather than remote off-road travel.

## Cost

No separate nightly camping fee, but nonresidents need the:

**$10 Conservation License**

## Camping Rule

Camping is not automatically allowed everywhere.

Unless posted otherwise:

**camp only in specifically designated or marked areas**

Typical rule:

**7 nights during any consecutive 30-day period**

## Vehicle Rule

In designated dispersed camping areas, vehicles may generally travel no more than:

**50 yards from a designated road**

where allowed.

In undeveloped camping areas:

- more than 100 feet from campsites
- more than 100 feet from trails

May only be dumped at designated facilities.

**December 2 through noon May 15**

Exact dates vary by area.

## Accent Rating

Varies widely.

Use designated parking/camp access only.

# Montana Hazard Guide

## 1. Grizzly Bears

Major concern in western and northwestern Montana.

Carry:

- bear spray
- secure food storage
- clean campsite habits

Never leave food unattended.

---

## 2. Black Bears

Widespread.

Use the same food-discipline system.

---

## 3. Wildfire

Serious summer and fall risk.

Prepare with:

- gas stove
- ABC fire extinguisher
- shovel
- current fire information

---

## 4. Smoke

Wildfire smoke can make an otherwise legal campsite unhealthy or unpleasant.

Be prepared to relocate.

---

## 5. Wet Clay / Gumbo

Especially dangerous in:

- CMR
- Missouri Breaks
- eastern badlands
- prairie BLM roads

If rain is forecast:

**leave before the clay turns wet**

---

## 6. Mountain Snow

High-elevation roads can receive snow:

- early fall
- late spring
- even during unusual summer events

Do not assume September guarantees snow-free mountain access.

---

## 7. Lightning

Common in summer mountain and prairie storms.

Avoid:

- ridge tops
- lone trees
- exposed high ground

---

## 8. Hail

Eastern and central Montana can get severe hail.

Protect:

- vehicle
- solar panels
- tent

---

## 9. High Wind

Especially common:

- Rocky Mountain Front
- Great Falls region
- central/eastern plains

Use strong shelter anchors.

---

## 10. Rattlesnakes

Present in many drier areas east of and within the Rockies.

Watch:

- sunny slopes
- rocks
- prairie dog towns
- brush

---

## 11. Ticks

Common statewide.

Perform frequent checks.

---

## 12. Mountain Lions

Present in many mountain regions.

Rarely seen, but remain aware in remote forest camps.

---

## 13. High Water / Runoff

Spring runoff can affect:

- rivers
- bridges
- low forest roads
- creek crossings

---

## 14. Deadfall

Wind and beetle-killed trees can create falling-tree hazards.

Never camp beneath visibly unstable dead trees.

---

## 15. Hunting Season

Fall overlaps with excellent camping weather.

Use:

- bright clothing
- visible campsite placement
- awareness around trailheads and hunting access

---

## 16. Weak Cell Service

Expect dead zones throughout:

- forests
- CMR
- Breaks
- remote BLM
- mountain valleys

Carry offline maps.

# Final Rule

When choosing a free Montana campsite, think in this order:

**land ownership → legal road access → road quality → weather → bear-storage order → fire restriction → water → sanitation → stay limit → supply town**

For a low-clearance front-wheel-drive car, add one more rule:

**If a Montana road is wet clay, marked high-clearance, snowed in, deeply rutted, or visibly turning into an OHV route, turn around early.**

Montana gives you enough legal public land that there is almost never a good reason to risk the car for one campsite.
