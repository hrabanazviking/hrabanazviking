- Truly free camping versus fee-based primitive camping
- Legal stay limits
- Permit requirements
- Human-waste disposal
- Campfire rules
- Ranger and agency contacts
- Road conditions
- 2014 Hyundai Accent SE accessibility
- Grocery, library, and gym access

- Seasonal weather
- Wildlife and environmental hazards
- Cellular reliability for Verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile

> **Important:** Campground status, WMA rules, hunt closures, road conditions, fire bans, permits, and seasonal access can change. Always verify with the managing agency immediately before travel.

# Agency-Wide Rules

## U.S. Forest Service — Francis Marion & Sumter National Forests

### Dispersed Camping
Francis Marion and Sumter National Forests require a **free visitor pass or permit for groups camping in undesignated areas**.

### Typical Stay Limit
About **14 days in a 30-day period**.

### Andrew Pickens Dispersed Camping Placement Rules
Current Forest Service rules require dispersed camps to remain approximately:

- 50 feet from lakes and streams
- 50 feet from maintained trails
- More than 1/4 mile from a roadway

This means Andrew Pickens dispersed camping is fundamentally **backpack camping rather than roadside vehicle camping**.

### Human Waste
Use Leave No Trace principles:

- Cathole roughly 6–8 inches deep where legal
- At least 200 feet from water
- At least 200 feet from camps
- At least 200 feet from trails
- Pack out toilet paper and hygiene products when possible

### Campfires
- Use established fire sites where permitted
- Use dead/down wood only
- Never leave a fire unattended
- Fire bans override normal rules

### Ranger Offices
- Andrew Pickens Ranger District: **864-638-9568**
- Enoree Ranger District: **803-276-4810**
- Long Cane Ranger District: **803-624-9471**
- Francis Marion Ranger District: **843-336-2200**
- Francis Marion alternate: **843-336-3248**
- Forest Supervisor: **803-561-4000**

## South Carolina Department of Natural Resources — SCDNR

### General Camping Rule
Camping on DNR properties is allowed only at designated campsites.

Unless a property-specific permit authorizes otherwise, the general maximum is approximately:

**4 nights in one location**

Groups of 10 or more generally require additional authorization.

### Campfires
Open fires are allowed only:

- At designated campsites
- Where not prohibited by current restrictions
- Using dead/down wood or permitted fuel

Never leave fires unattended.

### Human Waste
SCDNR does not provide a universal blanket rule allowing catholes across all properties.

**Best nomad practice:**
- Use a portable toilet
- Use WAG bags
- Pack waste out
- Only bury waste where property-specific rules explicitly permit it

### Useful DNR Contacts
- Heritage Preserves: **803-734-3893**
- Wildlife Management Areas: **803-734-3886**
- Region 1 Clemson: **864-654-1671**
- Region 2 Florence: **843-661-4766 / 843-661-4767**
- Region 4 Charleston Wildlife: **843-953-9856**

# South Carolina Fire-Season Warning

South Carolina's highest wildfire danger generally occurs:

**January through mid-April**

## State Forester's Burning Ban
May prohibit recreational campfires.

## Red Flag Alert
Warns of high fire danger but is not always itself a legal fire ban.

Always verify current fire status before using a campfire.

# Hyundai Accent Rules for South Carolina

1. **Do not take the Accent onto Jocassee interior jeep roads.**
2. Use only the official parking approach at Burrell's Ford.
3. Do not drive through Lowcountry water if the bottom is invisible.
4. Scout red-clay roads after several days of rain.
5. Turn around when sugar sand shows deep tire trenches.
6. Download Forest Service MVUMs and offline maps.
7. Verify bridge, gate, and culvert conditions before committing.
8. Never let a navigation app override visible road reality.

> **The easiest recovery is the one you never need.**

# Best Overall Takeaway

For a nomad living from a **2014 Hyundai Accent**, South Carolina is strongest in three layers:

## Best for Daily Digital Nomad Life
- Santee Coastal Reserve
- Elmwood
- Honey Hill
- Enoree

## Best for Balanced Wilderness + Logistics
- Francis Marion
- Long Cane
- Waccamaw River area

## Best for Deep Solitude
- Burrell's Ford
- Andrew Pickens
- Jocassee
- Coastal islands

For winter travel, the **McClellanville / US 17 / Francis Marion corridor** is probably the strongest South Carolina cluster because it combines:

- Free primitive camping
- Mild winter climate
- Low road grades
- Good resupply access
- Nearby libraries and gyms
- Stronger Verizon coverage
- Multiple legal camping systems within the same general region
